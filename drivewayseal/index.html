About this tool

A driveway takes a beating. Sun, rain, frost, oil drips and tyres all wear away at asphalt and concrete, and a good coat of sealer is the cheapest protection available. Sealer fills hairline cracks, restores a dark, even colour, and creates a barrier that keeps water out of the surface. But buying the right amount matters: too little leaves the job half done, and too much leaves you with open cans that harden before the next coat is due. A sealer calculator removes that guesswork.

DriveSeal is a free driveway sealer calculator. Enter your driveway's length and width, or its total area if you already know it, choose how many coats you plan to apply, and enter the coverage printed on your sealer product. The tool calculates how many litres you need, how many cans that means for common can sizes, and an estimated total cost. It is not a room paint calculator: driveway sealer has different coverage, different coats and different preparation, and this tool is built for driveways specifically.

Measuring the driveway correctly is the first step, and it is simpler than it looks. For a rectangular driveway, multiply length by width to get the area. For an L-shaped or irregular driveway, split it into rectangles, measure each one, and add the areas together. DriveSeal lets you enter length and width directly, or skip straight to area if you have already done the arithmetic. Measuring in metres keeps the maths clean, because most sealer coverage is printed in square metres per litre, and mixing feet and metres is the most common source of error.

Coverage is the number that decides everything, and it varies more than buyers expect. A typical asphalt sealer covers around 8 to 12 square metres per litre on a smooth surface, but a rough, porous or previously unsealed surface can drink far more product on the first coat. Concrete sealers often cover a wider range, and the product label is the authority: always use the coverage printed on the can you actually bought, not a figure remembered from a previous job. DriveSeal uses your product's coverage for both coats, and it is worth entering a lower coverage for the first coat if your surface is rough.

Two coats are standard for most driveways, and the reason is durability rather than appearance. The first coat soaks in and binds the surface, while the second coat builds an even film on top. Applying a single thick coat instead of two thin ones is a false economy: thick coats take longer to dry, can peel, and often crack as they cure. DriveSeal calculates both coats separately in its total, so you can see exactly how much of your purchase goes to each coat, and why skipping the second coat saves less than it seems.

Wastage deserves a small allowance, and DriveSeal includes it transparently. Edges, overlaps, spillage and product left in the can typically add around 10 percent to the theoretical quantity, and a driveway with many edges or a steep slope may need slightly more. The calculator adds a 10 percent allowance to the raw figure and rounds up to whole litres, so you are not left a litre short with a brush in your hand and the shops closed. Buying one extra litre is far cheaper than a second trip, or a half-sealed driveway left to weather.

Can sizes turn litres into a shopping list. Sealers are commonly sold in 10, 20 and 25 litre cans, and the right combination depends on your total. DriveSeal shows how many cans of your chosen size you need, rounded up, so you can compare the price of one large can against two smaller ones. Larger cans are usually cheaper per litre, but a can you cannot lift or store safely is no bargain. Choose the size you can actually handle, and remember that opened sealer does not keep indefinitely, so buying far more than you need is waste, not insurance.

Cost estimation helps you budget before you start, and it also helps you compare doing the job yourself against hiring a contractor. Enter the price per litre, or the price per can, and DriveSeal estimates the material cost of the job. Labour, crack filler, cleaning products and tools are separate, so the estimate is for sealer alone, but that clarity is useful: if a contractor's quote is several times the material cost, you can judge whether their preparation, labour and guarantee justify the difference, rather than guessing at the whole figure.

Preparation decides whether sealer lasts two years or five, and no calculator can substitute for it. Clean the driveway thoroughly, remove oil stains with a degreaser, fill cracks wider than a hairline, and let the surface dry completely. Sealer applied over dirt, oil or damp patches will peel, no matter how carefully you measured. DriveSeal assumes a clean, dry, prepared surface in its coverage figures, which is another reason to follow the product label: a surface that needs extra preparation may also need extra product on the first coat.

Weather and timing matter as much as measurement. Most sealers need a dry surface, air temperatures above around 10 degrees Celsius, and at least 24 hours without rain to cure. Sealing in the heat of midday can cause the product to dry too fast and leave lap marks, while sealing late in the day risks dew settling on a wet coat. Plan for a dry window of two days for two coats, keep vehicles off the surface for at least 48 hours, and check the label for the exact curing time of your product.

Concrete and asphalt driveways need different products, and using the wrong one is a costly mistake. Asphalt sealers are typically coal-tar or asphalt-emulsion products designed to flex with the surface, while concrete sealers are acrylic or penetrating products designed for a harder, less flexible base. DriveSeal works for either surface as long as you enter the correct coverage, because the arithmetic is the same, but you must buy the product made for your driveway type. If you are unsure which surface you have, a local supplier can identify it from a photo or a sample.

Maintenance keeps a sealed driveway looking good for years. Sweep regularly, clean oil spills promptly before they soften the sealer, and avoid turning steering wheels while stationary, which scuffs the surface. Most driveways need resealing every two to three years, depending on climate and traffic, and the first sign is usually a faded, grey appearance or water soaking in rather than beading on the surface. When that happens, measure again with DriveSeal, because the second sealing may need less product than the first, as the surface is now less porous.

Start by measuring your driveway today, before you buy anything. Enter the dimensions, choose two coats, and read the coverage from a product you are considering. The result is your shopping list, your budget and your plan in one place, and it takes less time than a trip to the shop and back. A well-sealed driveway protects a surface that would cost far more to replace than to maintain, and getting the quantity right is the simplest part of doing the job well.

Autumn is a popular sealing season, and for good reason: temperatures are mild, rain is often less intense than in spring, and the surface has a full winter ahead in which protection matters most. Seal in early autumn rather than late, so the product cures fully before the first frost, and avoid sealing over leaves or debris that will stick to the wet coat and spoil the finish. If autumn weather turns against you mid-job, stop after a completed coat rather than rushing a second coat onto a damp surface, because a single good coat that cures properly protects far better than two coats applied in a hurry.

Frequently asked questions

How much sealer do I need?

Divide your driveway area by the product coverage, multiply by the number of coats, and add about 10 percent for wastage. DriveSeal does this arithmetic for you.

How many coats should I apply?

Two thin coats are standard. The first coat soaks in and binds the surface, and the second builds an even protective film.

What coverage should I enter?

Use the coverage printed on your product label, in square metres per litre. Rough surfaces need a lower coverage figure than smooth ones.

Can I use DriveSeal for a room paint job?

No. DriveSeal is built for driveway sealer, with driveway coverage, coats and can sizes. Room paint has different coverage and preparation.

How long before I can drive on it?

Keep vehicles off for at least 48 hours, and check your product label for its exact curing time and rain window.

Is DriveSeal free?

Yes. DriveSeal is free with unlimited usage and no sign-up.
