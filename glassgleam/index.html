Window Cleaning Solution Calculator

Streak-free method: wash with the solution, squeegee top to bottom in overlapping strokes, and wipe the blade after every pass. Clean in the shade — solution drying in direct sun is the number one cause of streaks.

Anyone who has ever mixed a bucket of window cleaner knows the two classic failures: far too much solution sloshing around at the end, or running dry halfway up the second floor. Both come from guessing. The window cleaning solution calculator replaces guessing with measurement. You enter how many windows you are cleaning and their average size, pick a mix strength, and it tells you exactly how much solution to make, how much of it is vinegar and how much is water, and how many cloths to have ready. The result is a batch sized to the job — enough to finish without waste, mixed in the right proportions so it actually cleans instead of smearing.

How to use this calculator

The calculator starts from your total glass area: window count multiplied by average window area in square feet. It then applies a simple coverage rule — about 10 millilitres of solution per square foot of glass — to get the total batch size. Your chosen mix strength splits that batch into vinegar and water: the standard 50/50 mix is the all-purpose default, the light 1:2 mix suits lightly soiled glass and delicate frames, and the gentle 1:3 mix is for frequent maintenance cleans. Cloths are estimated at one per three windows, since a damp cloth and a dry buffing cloth in rotation keep you from reusing a saturated cloth on clean glass.

  1. Count every window you plan to clean, including small bathroom and skylight windows that are easy to forget.
  2. Estimate the average window area — a typical casement window is around 12 to 18 square feet; measure one or two if you are unsure.
  3. Choose a mix strength: standard for grimy glass, light for dusty glass, gentle for regular upkeep.
  4. Press Calculate and read your batch size, vinegar and water amounts, and cloth count.
  5. Mix in a labelled spray bottle or bucket, and keep the solution off stone sills and painted frames where vinegar can mark.

Understanding your results

Total solution. The batch size in millilitres, sized to your total glass area. One litre covers roughly 100 square feet at this coverage rate.

Vinegar and water split. Your mix strength applied to the batch. Vinegar cuts grease and mineral deposits; water carries it and dilutes its acidity.

Cloth count. A practical estimate so you are not wringing out a soaked cloth on the last windows. Add a squeegee for panes larger than a square foot or two.

Zero results. If you entered zero windows, the batch is zero — the calculator will not invent solution for a job that does not exist.

Practical tips

  • Clean in the shade or on a cloudy day so the solution does not dry on the glass before you squeegee it off.
  • Wipe your squeegee blade after every stroke — a dirty blade is a streak machine.
  • Do the dirtiest windows last so your solution stays clean for the glass that shows most.
  • Use distilled water if your tap water is hard; mineral deposits from hard water cause spots that look like streaks.
  • Replace cloths once they are saturated — a wet cloth pushes dirt around instead of lifting it.
  • Clean frames and sills first, then the glass, so dirty runoff does not land on finished panes.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Mixing a huge bucket for a small job and pouring most of it away.
  • Using neat vinegar on glass — it is unnecessary, it smells, and it can damage seals and stone.
  • Cleaning in direct sun and blaming the recipe for the streaks.
  • Reusing one cloth for the whole house until it is grey and gritty.
  • Forgetting skylights, fanlights and small panes when counting windows — then running out halfway.
  • Spraying solution onto hot glass, where it evaporates before it can dissolve anything.

If you clean glass more than once a year, a sized batch saves money on vinegar and cloths and saves the far more valuable resource — your time on a ladder. Worked example

Take a family home with 12 windows averaging 15 square feet each. Total glass area is 180 square feet, so the batch is 1,800 millilitres at the standard mix: 900 millilitres of vinegar and 900 millilitres of water, with about 4 cloths. Compare a small flat with 4 windows at 12 square feet: 48 square feet gives a 480 millilitre batch — 240 millilitres each of vinegar and water — and 2 cloths. Mixing the family-home batch for the flat would waste more than a litre of solution; mixing the flat batch for the house would run out before the third room.

Checking and re-running your result

Verify your batch by checking the first window: the glass should wet evenly without rivers of solution running to the sill. If you consistently finish with solution left over, reduce your average window area estimate; if you run dry, increase it. Save the result to history each time you clean, and after two or three cleans you will know your home's true batch size by heart — and you can re-run the calculator in seconds whenever you add windows, move house, or take on a shopfront. Technique and timing matter as much as the recipe, and a few refinements separate a good clean from a flawless one. Start with water quality: if your area has hard water, the minerals left behind as droplets dry will spot the glass no matter how good your mix is, so distilled or deionised water is worth the small cost for the final rinse on large panes. Work in a consistent pattern — top to bottom, left to right — so you never re-clean a finished section or lose track of which panes are done. For exterior glass, a squeegee with a fresh rubber blade is the single best investment; replace the rubber when it starts to chatter or leave lines, which is usually every few months of regular use. Safety matters too: never lean from a ladder to reach a far corner, and for high windows consider a water-fed pole or a professional rather than a risky stretch. Clean on a schedule rather than waiting for grime to build — quarterly exterior cleans and monthly interior touch-ups keep the job short, because light soiling needs the gentle mix and less scrubbing. Finally, keep a small kit ready: your measured spray bottle, two microfibre cloths, a squeegee and a bucket, so the job starts in minutes instead of after a hunt through cupboards. Small systems like this are what turn a dreaded chore into a twenty-minute routine. A quick reference for batch sizes keeps the job fast: a small flat with four average windows needs under half a litre of solution; a typical family home with twelve windows needs just under two litres; and a shopfront with thirty larger panes needs around six litres. Mix only what the calculator recommends, label the bottle with the ratio you used, and store any leftover solution away from children and pets, since even a mild vinegar mix is unpleasant in eyes and should never be transferred into a food or drink container.

Frequently asked questions

How much solution do I need per window?

About 10 millilitres per square foot of glass, so a typical 15-square-foot window needs roughly 150 millilitres.

What is the best vinegar-to-water ratio for windows?

A 50/50 mix is the standard all-purpose ratio. Use a lighter 1:2 mix for lightly soiled glass and a gentle 1:3 mix for frequent maintenance.

Can I use vinegar on all windows?

Vinegar is safe on glass, but keep it off natural stone sills, damaged seals and some tinted films. Rinse frames after cleaning.

Why do my windows streak?

The usual causes are cleaning in direct sun, a dirty squeegee blade, hard tap water, or a saturated cloth. Fix those before changing your recipe.

Is distilled water really better?

Yes, if your tap water is hard. Minerals in hard water dry into spots that look exactly like streaks.

How many cloths do I need?

Plan on one cloth per three windows, plus one dry buffing cloth. Large panes cleaned with a squeegee need fewer cloths.
