The FLAMES Love Game: How a Childhood Pastime Became the Internet's Favorite Compatibility Test

Long before dating apps, compatibility quizzes, and astrology memes, there was a folded piece of notebook paper, a borrowed pen, and six mysterious letters: F-L-A-M-E-S. If you grew up anywhere in the world between the 1970s and the 2000s, you almost certainly played it — during a boring math class, at a sleepover, or on a long bus ride. Two names went in, common letters were crossed out with theatrical seriousness, and the universe (or at least the playground) delivered its verdict: Friendship, Love, Affection, Marriage, Enemy, or Sibling.

Decades later, the FLAMES game refuses to die. It has survived the jump from paper to pixels, from schoolyard whispers to viral social posts, and it remains one of the most searched love games on the internet. AmoraMatch exists to give this beloved ritual the stage it deserves: a candlelit, animated theater where every crossed-out letter and every elimination round plays out before your eyes — plus a love score, a couple-type label, and an optional zodiac overlay that the paper version never had.

What Exactly Is the FLAMES Game?

FLAMES is an acronym for six relationship outcomes: Friendship, Love, Affection, Marriage, Enemy, and Sibling. The game takes two names — yours and your crush's, your partner's, or honestly anyone's — and runs them through a two-phase ritual of cancellation and elimination. The surviving letter is treated as your playful destiny together.

Nobody knows precisely where FLAMES was invented. Like many playground traditions, it seems to have emerged organically and spread by imitation, with strong roots in South Asia, the Middle East, and parts of Europe. Some trace its popularity to the 1970s and 1980s, when it traveled through school notebooks the way memes travel through group chats today. What is certain is that the game filled a universal human need: a low-stakes, slightly magical way to ask the terrifying question, "Do they like me back?"

The genius of FLAMES is that it is deterministic. Given the same two names, the classic rules always produce the same result — which is exactly why friends could argue about the "right" way to count, and why the game felt like it was revealing something real rather than rolling dice. AmoraMatch honors that determinism: our love score uses a fixed hash formula, so the same pair of names always earns the same score, and a shared result card will match what your partner sees.

How the FLAMES Calculation Actually Works

The ritual has two acts, and AmoraMatch animates both of them in its Candlelit Theater.

Act I: Letter Cancellation

Both names are lowercased and stripped of spaces and non-letters. Then, working through the first name letter by letter, each letter that also appears in the second name is cancelled — one occurrence from each side. In the classic playground version you would physically strike through matching pairs: the J in John and the J in Jane, the N and the N, until no more pairs remain. Whatever letters survive on both sides are counted together. That total is the magic number for Act II.

Act II: The Elimination Count

Write down F-L-A-M-E-S. Starting from F, count forward by the magic number; the letter you land on is eliminated. Continue counting from the next surviving letter, eliminate again, and repeat until a single letter stands alone. If your count is 4, for example, the first pass removes M (F-1, L-2, A-3, M-4), the next pass starting from E removes L, then F, then A, then S — leaving E: Enemy. Try it on paper sometime; watching the circle shrink is half the fun, and it is exactly what our animated theater recreates with glowing tiles.

A few quirks worth knowing: if the two names share every letter (identical names, for instance), the remaining count is zero and the classic game has no move — AmoraMatch playfully calls this a Mirror Match. And a remaining count of 1 always ends on S, because the count lands on the starting letter every single round.

The Love Score: Where Does the Percentage Come From?

Traditional FLAMES gives you a word, not a number — but modern players love a percentage they can screenshot. AmoraMatch computes a love score from 0 to 100 using a deterministic hash of the two names (the FNV-1a algorithm, for the technically curious). Because the names are sorted before hashing, the order you type them in does not matter, and because hashing is fixed, the same pair always gets the same score.

Why does determinism matter? Two reasons. First, it preserves the soul of the original game: FLAMES was never random, and your score should not be either. Second, it makes sharing honest — when you send your result card to your partner and they run the same names, they will see the identical number. No "re-roll until it looks good" trickery, just one shared fate to laugh about.

Let us be completely clear, though: the love score measures nothing real. It does not analyze your personalities, your texts, or your star charts. It is a fixed mathematical transformation of two strings of letters, dressed up in candlelight. Treat a 97% the way you would treat a fortune cookie — delightful, not decisive.

The Zodiac Overlay: What the Stars Add

Birthdays are optional on AmoraMatch, but adding both unlocks a zodiac compatibility overlay. We derive each person's Western sun sign from standard date ranges — Aries begins March 21, Taurus April 20, and so on through Pisces — and then compare the signs' classical elements: Fire, Earth, Air, and Water.

The element logic follows the oldest rules in astrology. Same-element pairs (two Fires, two Waters) speak the same emotional language. Fire and Air is the classic power couple — air feeds fire. Earth and Water nourish each other like rain on soil. The spicier combos are Fire and Water, which can steam or scald, and Earth and Air, which often want different things at different speeds. Fire–Earth and Air–Water sit in the middle: workable, interesting, occasionally exasperating.

Same-sign couples get their own note, because dating your mirror image is its own special adventure — instant understanding, doubled strengths, and yes, doubled drama. As with everything here, read it as entertainment with a wink, not as relationship advice.

The Honest Truth: Why We Love Games Like This

Psychologists have a name for part of the appeal: the Barnum effect, our tendency to find personal meaning in vague or playful statements. There is also simple nostalgia — for many players, FLAMES is a direct line back to being twelve years old and deliciously nervous. And there is the social function: a game gives two people permission to talk about feelings they would never raise directly. "The game says Marriage" is a much safer sentence than "I think about marrying you."

None of that makes the game "real," and AmoraMatch will never claim otherwise. Every result page carries a clear for-entertainment-only disclaimer, and we mean it. Real compatibility is built from conversations, kindness, shared values, and showing up on hard days — none of which fit inside an acronym. Use FLAMES as an icebreaker, a party trick, or a sweet little ritual with someone you like. Then put the phone down and go have the actual conversation.

F = Friendship, L = Love, A = Affection, M = Marriage, E = Enemy, S = Sibling. After the common letters are cancelled and the elimination count runs its course, the one surviving letter is your playful destiny for that pair of names.
