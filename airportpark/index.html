About ParkFly — airport parking cost calculator

Airport parking looks simple, a daily rate multiplied by days, until the details arrive: booking fees, shuttle times, covered versus open lots, minimum charges, partial days and cancellation terms. ParkFly starts with the honest total, days times daily rate plus any fee, because that total is the number to compare with a taxi, a rideshare, a friend's lift or public transport. A lot that advertises a low daily rate but adds a booking fee, a shuttle surcharge or a minimum stay can cost more than a slightly dearer lot with no extras, especially on a short trip. On a long trip the daily rate dominates, and a small difference per day becomes a large difference over two weeks. Comparing totals, not headlines, is the whole skill of choosing airport parking.

Trip length needs care, because airports often charge by calendar days or by 24-hour periods, and the two methods differ. If you park at 10 in the morning and return at 2 in the afternoon three days later, some lots charge three days and others charge four, because the final partial day counts. Check whether your lot uses 24-hour periods, calendar days, or a grace period of an hour or two, and enter the number of chargeable days, not just the number of nights away. Early-morning departures and late-night returns are where surprises happen, so add a buffer day in your comparison if your return flight could slip into the next charging period. ParkFly lets you enter any day count, so you can test both the planned and the delayed return.

Location within the airport system changes both price and stress. On-airport lots, especially short-stay and terminal-adjacent parking, are convenient but expensive, and they suit short trips, tight connections and travellers with heavy bags or limited mobility. Off-airport lots are usually cheaper and include a shuttle, but the shuttle adds time, waits in bad weather, and a transfer with luggage. A lot ten minutes further away may save money on a leisure trip with a comfortable connection, but it can turn a tight business trip into a sprint. When comparing, add shuttle time and frequency to the price: a free shuttle every five minutes is a different proposition from a paid shuttle every thirty minutes, and ParkFly's fee field can hold a shuttle charge if one applies.

Covered, secure and valet options each add cost for a specific benefit, and whether they are worth it depends on your trip and car. Covered parking protects from sun, hail, snow and bird droppings, which matters for a long trip in extreme weather or for a car you care about. Secure lots with fencing, lighting, cameras and patrols reduce, though never eliminate, the risk of theft or damage, and they are worth considering for overnight and multi-week trips. Valet parking saves walking and searching, but it means handing keys to a stranger and trusting their driving, and it usually costs the most. Open, basic lots are fine for a short trip in mild weather with nothing valuable left visible in the car, and they are often the cheapest honest choice.

Booking in advance usually saves money and guarantees a space, but the terms matter. Advance rates are often cheaper than drive-up rates, especially at busy airports in holiday periods, and booking removes the risk of arriving to a full lot. Cancellation terms vary widely: some bookings are fully flexible, some allow changes up to a few hours before arrival, and some are non-refundable, which is a poor choice if your flight could change. Loyalty schemes, credit-card parking benefits and airline partnerships can also cut the price, so check them before booking, and keep the booking confirmation and lot address offline, because airport mobile signal is not always reliable when you need directions most. Screenshot the shuttle pickup point as well as the lot location.

Comparing parking with alternatives requires the full journey cost, not just the parking total. A taxi or rideshare has a fare each way, surge pricing at peak times, and no parking risk, but it leaves you dependent on availability for the return journey. Public transport is often cheapest, but it may not run for early or late flights, and it is awkward with heavy bags, children or a large group. A friend's lift is free but costs someone else time and fuel, and a hotel park-and-fly package, where a night's stay includes parking for the trip, can beat separate parking and hotel costs for an early flight. Add fuel and tolls to the driving option, and remember that driving yourself also means arriving rested enough to drive home safely after a long flight.

Safety and practical tips round out a good parking choice. Remove valuables and documents from view, lock the car, note the lot section and bay number with a photo, and check the car for damage on return before leaving the lot, because claims are harder once you have driven away. Keep keys, booking details and identification in hand luggage, not in the parked car. If you return to a flat battery or a problem, know the lot's assistance number before you travel. For electric cars, check whether the lot offers charging, and do not rely on it for a critical return charge without confirming availability. A little preparation turns airport parking from a source of pre-flight anxiety into a routine part of the trip.

Worked examples show the total clearly. Three days at 12 per day with no fee costs 36 in total, a straightforward short-trip figure. Seven days at 10 per day plus a 5 fee costs 75, showing how a fee adds to a longer stay without dominating it. One day at 18 with no fee costs 18, the kind of premium short-stay price that suits a single-day trip but punishes a long one. Enter your own days, rate and fee, compare the total with your alternatives, and book the option that balances price, shuttle time and peace of mind for your specific flight.

Electric cars, accessibility and long-stay security each add a layer to airport parking that a simple daily rate does not capture, and checking them before booking avoids unpleasant surprises. Some airport lots now offer electric charging, but spaces are limited, charging may be slow, and a charger occupied by another car on your return is not a reliable plan, so confirm availability, cost and whether you need to book a charging bay separately. Accessible parking is usually located closest to terminals and shuttles, with wider bays and step-free routes, and travellers with a blue badge or equivalent should check eligibility, proof requirements and whether accessible spaces can be reserved, because they fill quickly at busy times. For trips of several weeks, ask about security patrols, lighting, fencing and vehicle checks, and consider whether a staffed lot with regular patrols is worth a premium over an open lot with cameras alone. Some travellers prefer to leave a car at a trusted friend's house or a hotel with long-stay parking, trading a little convenience for peace of mind and a car that is checked regularly. Before a long trip, service the car, check tyre pressures, leave it with enough fuel or charge for the drive home, and remove anything that could be damaged by heat or cold in a parked car, including medicines, electronics and perishables. On return, walk around the car before driving away, check tyres and lights, and report any damage to the lot operator immediately, with photos and your booking reference, because claims made after leaving the site are much harder to prove. A calm, prepared return journey is the final part of a good trip, and parking choices made before departure shape it more than most travellers expect.

Frequently asked questions

How is airport parking cost calculated?

Multiply chargeable days by the daily rate, then add any booking or shuttle fee. ParkFly gives that total so you can compare lots fairly.

Do airports charge by calendar day or 24 hours?

It varies by lot. Some use 24-hour periods, others calendar days, and some add a grace period. Check your lot's terms and enter chargeable days.

Is off-airport parking worth it?

Often yes for longer trips, if the shuttle is frequent and reliable. For tight connections or heavy bags, closer on-airport parking may be worth the premium.

What is park-and-fly?

A hotel stay that includes parking for your trip. For an early flight it can beat paying separately for a hotel and parking.

Should I book parking in advance?

Usually yes: advance rates are often cheaper and guarantee a space at busy times. Check cancellation terms before booking a non-refundable rate.

Is covered parking worth the extra cost?

For long trips in extreme weather or for a car you want to protect, yes. For a short trip in mild weather, open parking is usually fine.
